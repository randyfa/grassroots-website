**Optional companion document.** This companion document equips volunteers to shift disengaged Republicans and independents toward a positive, conservative perspective grounded in the 10 Commandments, the U.S. Constitution, and the Bill of Rights. It draws on ideas from the Heritage Foundation and Hillsdale College online courses. It is optional and secondary to the listen-first local model. Use it primarily in Republican club or ground-network settings after the other person opens the door on parties, values, or the next election. Always return to one local next step and the “We are the they” close. Frame remains: Return to the original social media — neighbors talking to neighbors. Match the ask to the setting.

## Positive Foundation
The 10 Commandments and natural law supply the moral baseline the Founders assumed. The Constitution and Bill of Rights secure ordered liberty: limited government, individual rights, consent of the governed, separation of powers, and federalism. Heritage’s True North principles and Hillsdale’s Constitution 101 emphasize that government exists to protect life, liberty, and property; free enterprise and strong families drive prosperity; and the Constitution’s original meaning matters. One practical bridge: “These are the ideas that built the freest and most prosperous country in history. Which of them still matter most to your family?”

## Clear Contrasts
* DSA (dsausa.org) platform calls for abolishing the police and prison system, abolishing the Senate, open borders and amnesty, public ownership of major industries, and replacing the presidential and Supreme Court structure with one subordinate to Congress. These conflict with the Constitution’s design.
* Islamist governance models prioritize religious law over individual rights and equal protection under the Constitution.
* Outlets such as CNN, MSNBC (MS NOW), ABC, CBS, and the Washington Post have repeatedly framed outcomes on inflation, border numbers, and crime in ways that diverge from primary data. Return to lived household results and primary sources.

## Lesser-of-Two-Evils Bridge
Use only after the other person raises “both sides are the same” or refuses to choose. Drawing on the practical distinction between a fallen candidate and an evil candidate: “Until Jesus returns, every candidate is fallen. The practical question is whether we are choosing a fallen person whose policies still protect the family and the Constitution, or someone whose policies lead whole communities into greater harm.” Fallen candidate: personal failings. Evil candidate (policy sense): actively advances outcomes that undermine ordered liberty, border security, or parental rights at scale. Close back to one local action.

## Short Bridge
*“The Constitution and the ideas behind it still protect families better than the alternatives. Which of those protections still matter most to you?”*

## The Issue and Expanded Bridge
**The issue**
Disengaged Republicans and independents often hear that both parties are the same or that national politics no longer matter. A positive conservative frame rooted in the Constitution, ordered liberty, and family results offers a clearer alternative than the DSA platform or Islamist models.
**Short background**
The Founders designed a limited government to protect God-given rights. Heritage and Hillsdale resources show how progressivism and modern socialism have moved away from that design. DSA proposals to abolish core institutions and Islamist prioritization of religious law over equal rights stand in direct contrast. Media framing from CNN, MSNBC, ABC, CBS, and the Washington Post frequently diverges from primary data on household costs and border numbers.
**The bridge**
“I hear the frustration. On the things that protect your family — costs, safety, schools, the border — the Constitution still gives us the best tools. Which of those is weighing on you most right now?” Listen, then offer one local next step. When appropriate: “Until Jesus returns, every candidate is fallen. The practical choice is between a fallen person whose policies still protect ordered liberty and someone whose policies lead communities into greater harm.”
*Close when it fits: “We are the they. What’s the one block or one person you’ll cover?”*

* Heritage True North principles and Hillsdale Constitution 101: original meaning, free enterprise, strong families, protection of life, liberty, and property.

**Contrasts**

**The perspective shift**
Local turnout still decides who protects or erodes these foundations. Precinct coverage, registration, and neighbors covering neighbors remain the practical path. The lesser-of-two-evils distinction is used only when the other person raises refusal to choose.

## 2-Minute Two-Person Conversation
*Setting: Front porch or sidewalk. Neighbor has just said both parties are the same or that voting doesn’t matter.*

**Neighbor:** Honestly, both sides are the same. I’m not sure any of it matters anymore.  
**You:** I hear that a lot. What’s hit your family the hardest lately — groceries, safety, schools, or something else?  
**Neighbor:** Groceries and the border. And I’m tired of the media telling me what to think.  
**You:** Yeah. The Constitution and the ideas behind it still protect families better than the alternatives. Groups like the DSA want to abolish the police, the Senate, and open the border. That is not the same as limited government under the Constitution. Outlets like CNN and the Washington Post often frame the numbers differently than what people see at the store.  
**Neighbor:** But every candidate has problems. Why choose?  
**You:** Until Jesus returns, every candidate is fallen. The practical question is whether we choose a fallen person whose policies still protect the family and the Constitution, or someone whose policies lead whole communities into greater harm. Local turnout is what decides that here.  
**Neighbor:** I’m not sure I want to get involved.  
**You:** No pressure. Would you be willing to check one neighbor’s registration or cover one block this month? I’ll follow up. We are the they. What’s one person you’d feel comfortable talking to?
