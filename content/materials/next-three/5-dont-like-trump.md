## Short Bridge
*“Fair enough. This conversation is about local results that affect your family, not national personalities. Which local issue is weighing on you most?”*

## The Issue and Expanded Bridge
**The issue**
Personal dislike of a national figure or of the party brand is a frequent reason independents and disengaged Republicans give for disengaging. The objection is often about identity or tone rather than about specific local results.
**Short background**
The Grassroots Conversations model deliberately does not lead with national framing. The goal is trust plus a practical next step on an issue the person already cares about. Costs, neighborhood safety, water with growth, housing, and school options can be discussed without requiring affection for any national figure or party label.
**The bridge**
“You don’t have to like anyone to care about costs, safety, and schools. Which of those is weighing on you and your family most right now?” Listen, mirror, and offer one local next step. Keep party language out unless they raise it.
*Ownership close: “We are the they. Who’s the next neighbor, the next person, you’ll talk to?”*

## Deeper Background
**Why the objection appears**
National figures and party brands dominate media. For many voters the brand has become a reason to opt out even when they still care about local results. The objection is real as an emotional barrier; it does not have to be the end of a useful conversation.
**Supporting context**

* Our training asks volunteers not to lead with national Trump framing unless the other person raises it.
* Kitchen-table issues (costs, safety, schools, water, housing) remain the highest-yield topics with reachable independents and disengaged Republicans.
* Local races and local policy on those issues do not require the voter to endorse any national figure.
* In mixed or civic settings the materials already keep language nonpartisan; party connection, if any, happens only in private follow-up.

**The perspective shift**
The person does not need to like a national figure or the party brand to care about a local result and take one practical step. The conversation stays on the issue they name. Affiliation can be discussed later if they choose; it is not a prerequisite for the next step.
**Ownership close**
“We are the they. What’s the one thing you’ll own?”

## 2-Minute Two-Person Conversation
*Setting: Front porch or sidewalk. Neighbor has just said they don’t like Trump or the GOP.*

**Neighbor:** I’ll be honest, I don’t like Trump. I don’t like the GOP.  
**You:** That’s fair. A lot of people feel that way, and I appreciate you being straight with me.  
**Neighbor:** So I’m not really interested.  
**You:** You don’t have to like anyone to care about costs, safety, and schools. Which of those is weighing on you and your family most right now?  
**Neighbor:** Schools. My kids are in school here.  
**You:** That gets decided locally, by the school board and the people who vote in those races. Would you be open to one practical step — checking your registration, or talking with one other parent about it?  
**Neighbor:** I could check my registration.  
**You:** That’s great. Thanks for talking with me. We are the they.

**Video script (one speaker, about 2 minutes)**
Sometimes you’ll hear, “I don’t like Trump,” or “I don’t like the GOP.” That’s common, and it’s often about national tone more than local results.
The bridge is direct: “You don’t have to like anyone to care about costs, safety, and schools.”
Then we ask which of those is weighing on them most. We listen. We mirror. We offer one practical local step. We do not lead with national framing unless they bring it up.
That’s the shift: from brand dislike to a concrete local concern and one thing they can do about it.
Neighbors talking to neighbors. We are the they.
