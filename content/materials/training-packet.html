The Six-Step Conversation

  1. Friendly opener. Introduce yourself as a neighbor.
  2. Ask an open question. Open on a local issue the person already cares about (costs, schools, safety, water, housing).
  3. Listen and acknowledge. Restate what you heard so they know you listened.
  4. Bridge. Link their concern to one practical local action.
  5. Soft close. Offer one next step (registration check, the next neighbor, the next person, or PC interest).
  6. Thank them. Stay gracious, even if they’re still frustrated.

Avoid leading with national framing.

“We Are the They” Close

Use after someone names a problem and points outward:

“I hear you. When we wait for them, three fingers point back at us. We are the they. Who’s the next neighbor, the next person, you’ll talk to this month?”

Write the commitment down. That is the tracking unit.

Match the Ask to the Setting

  • Republican clubs: Direct training, PC asks, and “We are the they” closes are appropriate. Use the full Grassroots Conversations frame.
  • Civic clubs (Rotary, Kiwanis, chambers, veterans posts): Nonpartisan briefing on registration dates and ballot information. Real conversations happen one-on-one afterward.
  • Churches and 501(c)(3) groups: Strictly nonpartisan. Registration table open to all is fine. No party language from the podium. Party asks only in private follow-up.

Ready-to-Use Scripts

1. Republican Club Session (5–7 minutes)

Open: “Grassroots Conversations: Return to the original social media — neighbors talking to neighbors. We have all heard the complaints about empty precincts. Today we are not talking about what someone else should do. We are talking about what we will do.”

Model: Quickly walk through the six steps with one local example (school board, water rates, or neighborhood safety).

Close: “We are the they. Before we leave, each of us names the next neighbor, the next person, or one civic group we will reach in the next 30 days. Write it down and tell the person next to you.”

Collect the commitments (name + one action) before people leave.

2. Civic / Church / Veterans Setting (nonpartisan)

Public open: “A lot of us are concerned about [local issue]. One practical step that helps is making sure people in our community are registered and know how to vote.”

Offer: Registration table or one-pager. Keep all party language out of the room.

Private follow-up later: “You mentioned [their concern]. Some of us are working through local Republican clubs to build better neighborhood coverage — neighbors talking to neighbors. Would you be open to hearing how that works?”

3. Equipping a Bridge Member (one-on-one)

“You already belong to [church / VFW / civic group]. That relationship is the bridge. Here is a short conversation card. Your job is simply to listen for the local concern, mirror it, and offer one practical next step. If someone wants to do more, note their name and I’ll follow up.”

4. Soft Close / Commitment Ask (any setting)

“Who is the next neighbor, the next person, you will talk to this month?”
Or: “We are the they. What is the one thing you will own?”

Simple Tracking

  • The tracking unit is a personal commitment: name + one action (the next neighbor, the next person, or one grassroots group).
  • Club leaders collect commitments at the end of trainings and keep a short list.
  • One volunteer keeps the master tracker for the clubs and their grassroots groups.
  • Update after every contact: last interaction, outcome/commitment, next follow-up date.
  • Monthly: each club’s liaison shares how many grassroots groups were identified, materials handed out, and names of interested people passed along for follow-up.

Connecting a Club With Its Precinct

A Precinct Committeeman can volunteer to connect one club with its precinct, supporting the club’s liaison and the personal bridges the club identifies. That volunteer:

  • Helps the club identify or confirm 3–5 grassroots groups where members already belong.
  • Equips the named bridge members with the conversation card, short scripts, and “We are the they” close.
  • Collects next-neighbor / next-person commitments and passes along the names of interested people.
  • Stays within match the ask to the setting: direct asks only in Republican club settings; nonpartisan in civic and church settings.

Participation is voluntary. There are no quotas. Success is measured by precinct coverage gains and practical local actions, not by the number of people recruited.

Success Looks Like

  • Every major club has a primary liaison and has named at least one grassroots group.
  • Bridge members are using the six steps and the neighbors-talking-to-neighbors frame.
  • Personal commitments are written down and followed up.
  • Names of people interested in becoming a PC or walking reach the people who can follow up.

Grassroots Team

Everyone on the path is part of the grassroots team supporting the grassroots network group — equipping personal bridges so neighbors can cover neighbors.

We are the they.

Personal responsibility. Practical local action. Consistent follow-through.
